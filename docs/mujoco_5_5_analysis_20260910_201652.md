## Method and scope

Timing and execution-stage comparisons exclude simulation time below 2 seconds. The retained new interval is 2.000–17.804 simulation seconds (15,804 control rows); baseline is 2.000–17.486 (15,486 rows). Outer running records are matched on simulation time and clipped to the controller interval. Controller time is its ROS elapsed clock, so endpoint alignment is approximate within clock delivery latency. Positive repeated clock stamps are retained (new: 0, baseline: 4). Stage occupancy is a fraction of logged control cycles, not measured wall time.

Full-recording rejection counts below are explicitly separate from warmup-filtered timing. The new recording covers approximately 17.8 simulation seconds plus its initial pause. This is one run per configuration, not a repeated controlled experiment. Human center ranges are comparable (x=0.45 m, z=0.30 m, y approximately -0.32–0 m), and final commanded path time is 8.558708292 s in both runs; timing, observation phases and intermediate robot states are not identical. Actual observation publish period is not recorded.

## Why worker computation varies

Across the complete new prediction log, actual rollout length ranges from 11 to 113 steps, mean 59.84, compared with 78.28 in old A. The fresh/committed intended container shrank from typically 28 to 10 commands, while braking length remains state-dependent. Rollout length versus worker compute has Pearson r=0.99735 in the new run. Monitor evaluation accounts for 87.4% of total worker elapsed time; planning and plan construction account for 6.7% and 4.8%.

Mean non-CPU time is only 0.001209 ms (about 0.118% of worker elapsed time), maximum 0.004681 ms. Most variation in this run is therefore associated with actual prediction workload, not thread waiting. Mean compute improves, but maxima and handoff p99 do not all improve; a single trial is insufficient to rank small tail differences.
